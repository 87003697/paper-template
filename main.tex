\begin{abstract}
  While 3D native generators have shown impressive results, they often produce semantically misaligned outputs in imaginative or stylized scenarios, resulting in geometric distortion or texture artifacts.
  To overcome this limitation, we propose \textbf{OREO}, which leverages pre-trained 2D diffusion priors to post-train 3D generators through an on-policy \textit{Render-Edit-Optimize} loop.
  At the core of OREO, the 2D prior is integrated through our \textbf{Reinforced Editing Distillation (RED)} algorithm. 
  It utilizes an enhanced FlowEdit strategy to generate \textbf{On-the-fly Pseudo-GTs} by editing rendered views from the 3D generator outputs. 
  These Pseudo-GTs serve as \textbf{dense supervision signals}, guiding the 3D generator towards semantic alignment while preserving the original geometric structure.
  Subsequently, we distill these signals into the 3D model via a \textbf{Contrastive Distillation Loss}. 
  Combined with \textbf{Trajectory Backpropagation}, OREO achieves end-to-end closed-loop optimization.
  Extensive experiments demonstrate that OREO significantly outperforms supervised baselines in handling complex creative inputs, achieving superior conceptual alignment and geometric integrity.
  \keywords{3D Generation \and On-Policy Distillation \and Post-Training}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Recently, 3D native generators such as Trellis and the Hunyuan3D series have become the mainstream approach for automating high-quality 3D content creation. 
This is due to their superior capabilities in producing high-fidelity geometry and detailed textures.
However, the performance of these pre-trained models is fundamentally constrained by the available 3D datasets, which remain at the million-scale due to the prohibitive costs of asset construction. 
Consequently, these datasets are limited in diversity, posing a significant challenge for creative applications like 3D concept design. 
In such scenarios, the input concepts are often highly stylized or imaginative, significantly deviating from the training distribution and resulting in perceptual misalignment, which manifests as geometric distortion or textual artifacts.

To address this challenge, we leverage the powerful priors of pre-trained 2D diffusion models, which benefit from training on billion-scale image datasets. 
Such extensive training equips them with robust perceptual understanding and superior capabilities in generating imaginative structures and intricate details, far surpassing the scope of existing million-scale 3D datasets. 
To effectively distill these rich 2D priors into 3D generators, we propose the \textbf{On-Policy Rendering Editing Optimization (OREO)} framework. 
The core idea is to establish an on-policy optimization mechanism: instead of learning from static datasets, the generator receives immediate feedback on its own artifacts from a 2D teacher. 
Specifically, OREO implements a \textit{Render-Edit-Optimize} loop. 
In this process, the model renders views from its current rollout, receives corrective edits from the 2D teacher, and uses these dynamic targets for model updates.

At the core of this loop is the \textbf{Reinforced Editing Distillation (RED)} algorithm, which generates corrective guidance through the sequential steps of \textit{view editing} and \textit{gradient distillation}. 
Specifically, for view editing, we employ an improved FlowEdit strategy to generate corrective edits. 
This process reinforces target concepts in a view-preserving manner, yielding view-consistent refined targets. 
Subsequently, to stably distill these signals, we propose a \textbf{contrastive loss} that leverages these targets to construct positive and negative sample pairs, achieving stable gradient updates. 
To optimize the 3D generator, OREO backpropagates the editing gradients calculated at the terminal state to every intermediate step of the entire generation trajectory.
  Extensive experiments on mainstream baselines, including Trellis and Trellis2, demonstrate that OREO significantly enhances their generalization capabilities on complex creative inputs.

In summary, our contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We introduce OREO, a novel post-training paradigm for 3D native generators via feedback from pre-trained 2D diffusion priors. 
    By establishing an on-policy Render-Edit-Optimize loop, it effectively compensates for 3D data scarcity, enabling robust generalization to highly stylized and imaginative concepts.
    \item To construct feedback from 2D priors, we propose the Reinforced Editing Distillation (RED) algorithm. 
    It generates corrective guidance by editing rendered views with an improved FlowEdit strategy and distilling these corrections into stable gradients via a contrastive loss.
    Additionally, OREO propagates these gradients to all intermediate steps of the generation trajectory to ensure effective optimization.
    \item We validate the effectiveness of OREO on mainstream baselines, including Trellis and Trellis2. Results show that our method significantly outperforms supervised baselines in both conceptual alignment and geometric fidelity on complex creative inputs
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

In this section, we present OREO, a post-training framework designed to generalize 3D native generators via on-policy optimization.
We first introduce the task formulation in Sec.~\ref{sec:problem_formulation} and preliminaries in Sec.~\ref{sec:preliminaries}.
We then detail our core Reinforced Editing Distillation (RED) algorithm, which consists of Reinforced Editing in Sec.~\ref{sec:reinforced_editing} and Contrastive Distillation in Sec.~\ref{sec:contrastive_distillation}.
Finally, we describe the Optimization process via trajectory backpropagation in Sec.~\ref{sec:optimization}.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}
Given a reference image $x^{ref}$, our goal is to optimize a 3D generator $G_\theta$ to synthesize a high-fidelity 3D asset.
We employ a differentiable renderer $\mathcal{P}$ to project the generated asset into 2D views from arbitrary camera poses $\pi$.
The optimization objective is to minimize the perceptual discrepancy $\mathcal{L}$ between the rendered views and the target concept:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:task}
\theta^* = \arg\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{x^{ref}, \pi} [\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P}(G_\theta(x^{ref}), \pi), x^{ref})]
\end{equation}
where the loss $\mathcal{L}$ is derived from our proposed on-policy distillation process.


\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}
\textbf{Flow Matching Framework and Notation.}
Flow Matching models generation as an ODE process that transports Gaussian noise to the data distribution:
\begin{equation}
    dx_t = v(x_t; t, c) dt, \quad t \in [1, 0]
\end{equation}
where $x_t$ is the latent state at time $t$, and $c$ is the condition (e.g., text or image).
For better alignment to the condition $c$, Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is applied to the velocity field, using a null condition $\emptyset$ and a guidance scale $w$:
\begin{equation}
    v^{w}(x_t; t, c) = v(x_t; t, \emptyset) + w \cdot (v(x_t; t, c) - v(x_t; t, \emptyset))
\end{equation}
A key property used in our method is single-step estimation of the clean sample $\hat{x}_0$ from the intermediate state $x_t$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:single_step_estimation}
    \hat{x}_0 = x_t - t \cdot v(x_t; t, c)
\end{equation}
To distinguish modalities, we use $z$ and $\theta$ for the 3D generator $v_\theta$, where $G_\theta$ denotes its ODE sampling process.
Similarly, we use $x$ and $\phi$ for the 2D editor $v_\phi$, where $\mathcal{E}_\phi$ denotes the multi-step editing process as described below.


\textbf{Inversion-Free Image Editing.}
We leverage FlowEdit, an inversion-free method that modifies images by coupling source and target flow trajectories.
Starting from $x^{src}$, it iteratively updates the editing variable via $x^{edit} \leftarrow x^{edit} - \Delta t \cdot \tilde{v}_t$, where the differential velocity is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:flowedit_transition}
    \tilde{v}_t = v_\phi^{w_{tgt}}(x_t^{tgt}; t, c^{tgt}) - v_\phi^{w_{src}}(x_t^{src}; t, c^{src})
\end{equation}
where the \textbf{source branch} and the \textbf{target branch} is constructed as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:flowedit_states}
    x_t^{src} = (1-t)x^{src} + t\epsilon, \quad x_t^{tgt} = x^{edit} + (x_t^{src} - x^{src}), \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I).
\end{equation}
The editing process is performed over the last $N_e$ steps of the full $N$-step ODE schedule.
With integration step size $\Delta t = t_{i-1} - t_i$, $x^{edit}$ evolves into $x^{tgt}$, which transfers the image from being consistent with $c^{src}$ to following $c^{tgt}$ without costly inversion, preserving structural fidelity.

\subsection{Constructing On-the-fly Pseudo-GTs via Reinforced Editing}
\label{sec:reinforced_editing}

To construct the loss function $\mathcal{L}$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:task}, we leverage the 2D editor $\mathcal{E}_\phi$ to refine the rendered view $x^{src}$ into a semantically aligned target $x^{tgt}$.
Specifically, we adapt the FlowEdit pipeline to reinforce the semantics of the reference $x^{ref}$ into the current view while preserving the original camera perspective, as illustrated in Fig.XXX.
We introduce two key modifications to achieve this, as follows.

\textbf{Differential Velocity Editing.}
Instead of transitioning from the source condition to the target condition as in Eq.~\ref{eq:flowedit_transition}, we use the same reference condition $c^{ref}$ for both branches and apply opposite guidance scales $w$ and $-w$.
The differential velocity $\tilde{v}_t$ is hereby defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:red_diff_velocity}
    \tilde{v}_t = v_\phi^{w}(x_t^{tgt}; t, c^{ref}) - v_\phi^{-w}(x_t^{src}; t, c^{ref}),
\end{equation}
which effectively drives the editing variable $x^{edit}$ towards the target concept without compromising the original layout.

\textbf{Dynamic Noise Update.}
At each editing step $t$, standard FlowEdit samples new noise to construct $x_t^{src}$ and $x_t^{tgt}$ as in Eq.~\ref{eq:flowedit_states}, and this stochasticity can dilute editing effectiveness.
We therefore update $\epsilon$ at each step via:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:red_dynamic_noise}
    \epsilon \leftarrow \epsilon - (v_\phi(x_t^{tgt}; t, c^{ref}) - v_\phi(x_t^{tgt}; t, \emptyset)) \cdot (1-t)
\end{equation}
which keeps the noise semantically focused and improves editing stability.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Reinforced Editing Distillation (RED)}
\label{alg:red}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Rendered view $x^{src}$, Reference condition $c^{ref}$, Editor $\mathcal{E}_\phi$, Guidance scale $w$, Editing steps $N_e$, Total steps $N$
\Ensure Target view $x^{tgt}$, Distillation Loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{RED}}$
\State Initialize $x^{edit} \leftarrow x^{src}$, Sample noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{RED}} \leftarrow 0$
\State Select time steps $\{t_i\}_{i=0}^{N_e}$ as the last $N_e$ steps from the full schedule of $N$ steps
\For{$i = 0$ to $N_e-1$} \Comment{\texttt{FlowEdit: Backward Euler Integration}}
    \State $t \leftarrow t_i$, $\Delta t \leftarrow t_{i+1} - t_i$
    \State $x_t^{src} \leftarrow (1-t)x^{src} + t\epsilon$
    \State $x_t^{tgt} \leftarrow x^{edit} + (x_t^{src} - x^{src})$ \Comment{\texttt{Eq.~\ref{eq:flowedit_states}}}
    
    \Statex \texttt{// Reinforced Editing (Sec.~\ref{sec:reinforced_editing})}
    \State $\tilde{v}_t \leftarrow v_\phi^{w}(x_t^{tgt}; t, c^{ref}) - v_\phi^{-w}(x_t^{src}; t, c^{ref})$ \Comment{\texttt{Eq.~\ref{eq:red_diff_velocity}}}
    \State $x^{edit} \leftarrow x^{edit} - \tilde{v}_t \Delta t$ \Comment{\texttt{FlowEdit: Euler Step}}
    \State $\epsilon \leftarrow \epsilon - (v_\phi(x_t^{tgt}; t, c^{ref}) - v_\phi(x_t^{tgt}; t, \emptyset)) \cdot (1-t)$ \Comment{\texttt{Eq.~\ref{eq:red_dynamic_noise}}}
    
    \Statex \texttt{// Contrastive Distillation (Sec.~\ref{sec:contrastive_distillation})}
    \State $x^{-} \leftarrow x_t^{src} - t \cdot v_\phi(x_t^{src}; t, \emptyset)$ \Comment{\texttt{Shared Negative Anchor}}
    \State $x^{+} \leftarrow x_t^{src} - t \cdot v_\phi^{w}(x_t^{src}; t, c^{ref})$ \Comment{\texttt{Source Positive Anchor}}
    \State $\mathcal{L}_{src} \leftarrow \Call{ContrastiveLoss}{x^{src}, x^{+}, x^{-}}$ \Comment{\texttt{Source Loss}}
    \State $x^{+} \leftarrow x_t^{tgt} - t \cdot v_\phi^{w}(x_t^{tgt}; t, c^{ref})$ \Comment{\texttt{Target Positive Anchor}}
    \State $\mathcal{L}_{tgt} \leftarrow \Call{ContrastiveLoss}{x^{src}, x^{+}, x^{-}}$ \Comment{\texttt{Target Loss}}
    \State $\mathcal{L}_{\text{RED}} \leftarrow \mathcal{L}_{\text{RED}} + (\mathcal{L}_{src} + \mathcal{L}_{tgt}) / N_e$ \Comment{\texttt{Eq.~\ref{eq:red_objective}}}
\EndFor
\State \Return $x^{tgt} \leftarrow x^{edit}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{RED}}$
\Statex
\Procedure{ContrastiveLoss}{$x, x^{+}, x^{-}$}
    \State $w_{ada} \leftarrow (\| x - x^{+} \|_1 + \eta)^{-1}$ \Comment{\texttt{Adaptive Normalization}}
    \State \Return $w_{ada} \cdot (\| x - x^{+} \|^2 - \| x - x^{-} \|^2)$ \Comment{\texttt{Eq.~\ref{eq:contrastive_objective}}}
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Distilling On-the-fly Pseudo-GTs via Contrastive Loss}
\label{sec:contrastive_distillation}

To distill the editing process into the 3D generator, we adopt a contrastive objective inspired by DMD.
For any noisy latent state $x_t$ (which can be $x_t^{src}$ or $x_t^{tgt}$), we define its positive and negative anchors via the single-step estimation in Eq.~\ref{eq:single_step_estimation}:
\begin{equation}
    x^{+} = x_t - t \cdot v_\phi^{w}(x_t; t, c^{ref}), \quad x^{-} = x_t - t \cdot v_\phi(x_t; t, \emptyset)
\end{equation}
The contrastive loss is then formulated as:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:contrastive_objective}
    \ell(x, x^{+}, x^{-}) = \frac{\|x - x^{+}\|^2 - \|x - x^{-}\|^2}{\|x - x^{+}\|_1 + \eta}
\end{equation}
We construct the final objective by combining a source loss $\mathcal{L}_{src}$ and a target loss $\mathcal{L}_{tgt}$, both sharing the negative anchor $x^{-}_{src}$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:red_objective}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{RED}} = \mathbb{E}_{t} [\underbrace{\ell(x, x^{+}_{src}, x^{-}_{src})}_{\mathcal{L}_{src}} + \underbrace{\ell(x, x^{+}_{tgt}, x^{-}_{src})}_{\mathcal{L}_{tgt}}]
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathcal{L}_{src}$ ensures stable optimization by anchoring to the source branch, while $\mathcal{L}_{tgt}$ drives effective semantic injection via the target branch.
The complete Reinforced Editing Distillation (RED) procedure is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:red}.

\subsection{Optimization}
\label{sec:optimization}


We optimize the 3D generator with the following procedure.
In each iteration, we first obtain a clean latent $z_0 = G_{\theta_{pre}}(x^{ref})$ from the frozen pre-trained generator.
We then perturb it with Gaussian noise at a random timestep $t \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)$ to yield $z_t = (1-t)z_0 + t\epsilon$.
Given $z_t$, the trainable generator predicts the velocity field $v_\theta(z_t; t, c)$, and the clean latent is estimated by
\begin{equation}
\hat{z}_0 = z_t - t \cdot v_\theta(z_t; t, c),
\end{equation}
which is then decoded and rendered with a sampled camera pose $\pi$ as $x^{src} = \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}(\hat{z}_0), \pi)$.
The loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{RED}}$ is computed on $x^{src}$, and gradients are backpropagated through the decoder and the denoising prediction to update $\theta$.
To preserve geometric stability, we regularize the velocity field deviation:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{reg} = \| v_\theta(z_t; t, c) - v_{\theta_{pre}}(z_t; t, c) \|^2
\end{equation}
where $v_{\theta_{pre}}$ is the velocity field induced by the frozen pre-trained generator.
The final objective is $\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{RED} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{reg}$.
We set $\lambda=10$ such that $\|\nabla_{v_\theta} \mathcal{L}_{RED}\| : \|\nabla_{v_\theta} \mathcal{L}_{reg}\| \approx 1:10$. The overall training procedure is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:oreo}.


\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{OREO Training Loop}
\label{alg:oreo}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Generator $G_\theta$, Frozen rollout generator $G_{\theta_{pre}}$, 2D editor $\mathcal{E}_\phi$, Dataset $\mathcal{S}$, Learning rate $\eta$
\Ensure Optimized parameters $\theta^*$
\While{not converged}
    \State Sample $x^{ref} \sim \mathcal{S}$, $\pi$, and $t \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)$
    \State $z_0 = G_{\theta_{pre}}(x^{ref})$ \Comment{\texttt{Rollout}: Generate clean latent}
    \State $z_t = (1-t)z_0 + t\epsilon,\ \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ \Comment{\texttt{Noise Injection}}
    \State Predict $\hat{z}_0$ from $z_t$ using $G_\theta$ \Comment{\texttt{Prediction}: Denoise latent}
    \State $x^{src} = \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}(\hat{z}_0), \pi)$ \Comment{\texttt{Render}: Decode and project}
    \State Obtain $\mathcal{L}_{\text{RED}}$ via Algorithm~\ref{alg:red} from $x^{src}$ and $x^{ref}$ \Comment{\texttt{Reinforced Editing}}
    \State $\mathcal{L}_{reg} = \| v_\theta(z_t) - v_{\theta_{pre}}(z_t) \|^2$ \Comment{\texttt{Regularization}}
    \State $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \nabla_\theta (\mathcal{L}_{\text{RED}} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{reg})$ \Comment{\texttt{Update parameters}}
\EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\textbf{Dataset.} To test our method on creative tasks, we collected a Conceptual Design Dataset. The training set has $\sim$2,000 high-quality concept images (like sci-fi vehicles and fantasy creatures) with complex shapes and unique textures, which are hard for existing 3D models to generate. The test set includes 100 new, highly imaginative design sketches.

\textbf{Baselines.} We compare OREO mainly with the original Trellis model to show the improvement over the supervised baseline.

\textbf{Metrics.} We use CLIP Similarity to check if the result matches the text description, and DINO Similarity to check if it looks like the reference image. We also ask human users to rate the quality of the geometry and textures.

\textbf{Implementation Details.}
We use Trellis-Image-Large as the 3D backbone and Qwen-Image-Edit for 2D feedback, rendering at $1024 \times 1024$ via Gaussian Splatting.
Training runs for 500 epochs on a single A800 GPU using SGD ($lr=5\times10^{-3}$, batch size 1, accumulation 4) with BF16 precision.
The contrastive distillation loss weight is set to 1.0.
More implementation details are provided in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Preliminary Analysis: Validating the Editing Feedback}

Before integrating the 2D editor into the 3D optimization loop, we first evaluate the quality of its feedback on an independent 2D benchmark.
The quality of 3D optimization is upper-bounded by the quality of pseudo-GTs: the editing residual $\Delta x$ should mainly reflect semantic correction rather than geometric destruction.


\textbf{Comparison with other feedback sources.}
We compare RED with two relevant image editing baselines: NanoBanana and original Qwen-Image-Edit.
Note that we do not compare against the original FlowEdit setup, since it assumes explicit source/target prompts while our setting only provides rendered views with the reference concept.
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:feedback_comparison}, NanoBanana tends to miss fine concept details, while original Qwen-Image-Edit introduces larger geometric drift under general-purpose editing.
RED achieves the best balance between semantic enhancement and structure preservation, which is critical for stable 3D supervision.

\textbf{Sensitivity analysis of RED parameters.}
After selecting RED as the feedback source, we further study its hyper-parameters.
We select 50 rendered images from the test set and report CLIP Similarity (concept alignment), DINO Similarity (feature consistency), and Silhouette IoU (contour overlap).
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:red_sensitivity}, we observe a clear trade-off between concept injection and geometric preservation, and choose Steps 9|12 with Cfg=4 as the final setting.

\textbf{Analysis and Conclusion}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Why RED as the Feedback}: Compared with NanoBanana and original Qwen-Image-Edit, RED preserves geometry significantly better while maintaining competitive semantic alignment. This property is crucial in 3D training because geometric drift in pseudo-GT views leads to conflicting multi-view gradients.
    \item \textbf{Choice of Guidance Scale}: Experiments show that excessively high CFG (e.g., 12) severely damages image quality, leading to drops in both CLIP and IoU. \textbf{Cfg=4} demonstrated the most robust performance, effectively enhancing conceptual features while maintaining high geometric consistency (IoU > 0.97).
    \item \textbf{Critical Role of Timestep Ratio}: We found that the starting point of the editing interval is more critical than the total number of steps. Comparing \textbf{Steps: 20|40 (Ratio 0.5)} and \textbf{Steps: 30|40 (Ratio 0.75)}, the latter brought nearly a \textbf{7x} improvement in CLIP (+0.0228 vs +0.0034). This indicates that at low noise levels like $t=0.5$, the image structure is solidified, and the model lacks sufficient \textbf{plasticity} to inject new concepts. Therefore, we fix the \textbf{Ratio to 0.75} to ensure sufficient editing freedom.
    \item \textbf{Efficiency-Quality Trade-off}: Given a Ratio of 0.75, we compared high-step (30|40) and low-step (9|12) strategies. Although 30|40 yielded slightly higher conceptual improvement, its Mask IoU dropped significantly (0.93), increasing the risk of geometric drift. In contrast, \textbf{Steps: 9|12} maintained extremely high geometric stability (\textbf{IoU $\sim$0.96}) while still achieving competitive conceptual enhancement, with a \textbf{70\%} reduction in computational cost.
\end{enumerate}

Based on the above analysis, we adopt the configuration \textbf{(Steps: 9|12, Cfg=4)} in all subsequent 3D experiments.

\subsection{Ablation Study and Analysis}

To deeply understand the contribution of each component of OREO, we conducted a series of ablation experiments.

\textbf{Analyzing Loss Components.}
We verified the necessity of each part of the Contrastive Distillation Loss (CDL).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Replace CDL with MSE}: We attempted to directly minimize the mean squared error ($\| x^{src} - x^{tgt} \|^2$) between the rendered image and the final output of FlowEdit. Results showed that although this strategy could quickly close the conceptual distance, it easily led to geometric distortion (e.g., uneven surfaces). This is because MSE forces the model to match the editing results at the pixel level, ignoring inevitable minor geometric deviations during the 2D editing process. In contrast, CDL utilizes relative gradient directions, providing more robust conceptual guidance.
    \item \textbf{Remove Repeller Term ($\| x^{src} - \hat{x}^{-}_{t \to 0} \|^2$)}: Retaining only the attractor term led to the model overfitting the editing target, ignoring the suppression of the unconditional distribution, resulting in generated textures that were too smooth and lacked detail.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Sampling Strategy Analysis.}
To balance training efficiency and performance, we adopted Multi-step Time Sampling (MTS Sampling).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fixed Timestep}: If optimization is performed only at a fixed noise level (e.g., $t=500$), the model struggles to handle diverse input distributions, resulting in insufficient texture detail after convergence.
    \item \textbf{MTS Sampling (Ours)}: To address the overfitting problem caused by fixed timesteps, we adopted a \textbf{Stratified Random Sampling} strategy. Unlike solving ODEs on a fixed discrete grid, we divide the entire time axis into continuous sub-intervals and uniformly sample a time step from each sub-interval in each iteration. This strategy ensures that the model covers continuous noise levels while maintaining the correctness of the Global Flow Trajectory, significantly enhancing robustness to minor time shifts.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper proposes \textbf{OREO}, a general post-training framework for 3D concept design scenarios. Addressing the generalization bottleneck of existing 3D generative models when handling highly stylized and non-standard geometric inputs, we innovatively introduce an enhanced editing mechanism based on FlowEdit. By constructing a \textbf{Contrastive Distillation Loss}, OREO successfully distills the rich conceptual priors contained in 2D foundation models into 3D generators. Experimental results show that our method significantly outperforms supervised baselines in conceptual consistency, geometric fidelity, and texture detail, providing an efficient new path for solving the data scarcity problem in 3D generation.

\textbf{Future Work.} Although OREO performs well, there is still room for further exploration. First, the current FlowEdit process has high inference costs; future work could explore more efficient distillation strategies (e.g., one-step editing). Second, we will attempt to extend OREO to more complex scene generation tasks, utilizing panoramic editing models to optimize large-scale 3D environments. Finally, combining with Large Multimodal Models (LMM) for more fine-grained interactive editing is also an exciting direction.
