\begin{abstract}
  Despite recent advancements in 3D generation, models often struggle to produce assets with high visual fidelity.
  To bridge this gap, we propose OREO, an alignment framework that enhances the realism of 3D generators by leveraging rich 2D diffusion priors.
  Instead of relying on static datasets, OREO establishes a dynamic optimization loop that produces on-the-fly pseudo ground truths.
  At its core, we introduce Reinforced Editing, which utilizes a 2D model to refine rendered views of the 3D output, injecting realistic textures and fine-grained details while preserving the original geometric structure.
  These refined views serve as high-quality supervision targets, enabling the 3D generator to learn from its own generated samples and progressively improve its visual quality.
  Experiments demonstrate that OREO effectively improves upon pre-trained baselines, producing 3D assets with enhanced visual realism.
  \keywords{3D Generation  \and Alignment \and Image Diffusion Prior}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Recent 3D generators, such as Trellis and the Hunyuan3D series, have greatly simplified the 3D content creation process.
However, despite their strong performance in geometry generation, achieving high visual fidelity remains challenging.
Generated assets often lack intricate textures and fine-grained details observed in real-world subjects.
This limitation largely stems from the scarcity of high-quality 3D data with diverse appearances, which pales in comparison to the vast visual knowledge encapsulated in billion-scale 2D imagery.
Consequently, 3D generative models may not fully capture the nuanced visual characteristics required for photorealistic rendering.

To bridge this gap, we propose to distill the rich visual priors of pre-trained 2D diffusion models into 3D generators.
These models encapsulate a deep understanding of visual fidelity and fine-grained details, far surpassing the scope of existing 3D datasets.
To this end, we introduce the On-the-fly Rendering Editing Optimization (OREO) framework.
The core idea is to establish a dynamic alignment mechanism: instead of learning from static datasets, the generator improves by learning from pseudo ground truths generated on-the-fly.
Specifically, OREO implements a \textit{Render-Edit-Optimize} loop, where the model renders views from its current output, receives corrective edits to enhance realism, and uses these refined views as supervision targets.

At the core of this loop is the Reinforced Editing algorithm, which generates high-quality supervision signals.
To ensure reliable guidance, Reinforced Editing prioritizes structure preservation, focusing on injecting realistic textures and fine-grained details while maintaining the original spatial layout.
Inspired by recent inversion-free editing techniques, this approach mitigates the unwanted geometric deviations common in standard editing methods, ensuring that the generated pseudo-GTs remain geometrically aligned with the underlying 3D mesh.
We integrate these refined targets into a closed-loop optimization process, where the generator minimizes the discrepancy between its rendered predictions and the enhanced pseudo-GTs.
Additionally, a geometric regularization term is employed to maintain structural stability during training.
Experiments on the state-of-the-art Trellis model demonstrate that OREO effectively improves its performance, producing 3D assets with superior visual realism.

In summary, our contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We introduce OREO, a novel alignment framework that leverages pre-trained 2D diffusion priors to enhance the fidelity of 3D generators through an on-the-fly optimization loop with dynamic pseudo ground truths.
    \item We propose the Reinforced Editing algorithm, which adopts an inversion-free strategy to inject realistic textures into rendered views while prioritizing structure preservation for robust 3D optimization.
    \item We validate the effectiveness of OREO on state-of-the-art 3D generators. Results show that our method effectively improves visual realism and texture richness while preserving geometric integrity.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\noindent\textbf{Post-training of Pretrained 3D Generators.}
As 3D generation has shifted from optimization-based pipelines to large-scale pretraining, post-training has become the key stage for adapting generic priors to target objectives. Recent 3D-native foundations are first pretrained on large synthetic 3D corpora~\cite{xiang2025structured,zhao2025hunyuan3d,ye2025hi3dgen,li2025triposg,guo2025hyper3d,yushi2025gaussiananything}, and then further tuned with task-specific data to improve geometric stability and appearance controllability. From a pipeline perspective, post-training is typically performed in either geometry--texture coupled or decoupled frameworks: coupled methods update geometry and texture jointly in a unified representation~\cite{chen20253dtopia,xiang2025structured,yushi2025gaussiananything,lin2025diffsplat}, while decoupled methods commonly constrain or freeze geometry first and adapt the texturing stage afterward~\cite{zhao2025hunyuan3d,ye2025hi3dgen,li2025triposg,li2025step1x,guo2025hyper3d,zhang2025bang}. This pretrain-then-post-train paradigm enables efficient specialization while preserving diversity from large-scale pretraining.

\noindent\textbf{Texture-centric Adaptation and Realism Alignment.}
To address the realism gap of pretrained generators, post-training research mainly follows two directions. One line directly fine-tunes 3D-native texturing models under 3D-consistency constraints~\cite{yu2024texgen,xiong2025texgaussian,liu2024texoct}; the other fine-tunes multi-view image generators on 3D data and then fuses generated views into UV textures~\cite{huang2025mv,liang2025unitex,bensadoun2024meta,fei2025pacture,he2025materialmvp}. Additional efforts leverage real-image supervision or material-map enhancement~\cite{Jiang2025ICCV,he2025materialmvp,fei2025pacture,huang2025material,fang2024make}, but are still limited by cross-view inconsistency, rendering-dependent improvements, or the scale and fidelity bottlenecks of real scanned 3D datasets~\cite{downs2022google,dong2025digital,brazil2023omni3d}. Overall, current post-training trends emphasize combining multi-view consistency, structure-aware constraints, and realistic detail supervision to produce stable, rendering-independent photorealistic 3D outputs.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We present OREO, an alignment framework that enhances 3D visual fidelity via on-the-fly optimization.
We first introduce the problem formulation in Sec.~\ref{sec:problem_formulation} and preliminaries in Sec.~\ref{sec:preliminaries}.
We then detail our core Reinforced Editing algorithm in Sec.~\ref{sec:reinforced_editing}, which generates high-quality pseudo ground truths.
Finally, we describe the closed-loop optimization process in Sec.~\ref{sec:optimization}.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}
Given a reference image $x^{\texttt{ref}}$, our goal is to optimize a 3D generator $G_\theta$ to synthesize high-fidelity 3D assets.
We employ a differentiable renderer $\mathcal{P}$ to project generated assets into 2D views from arbitrary camera poses $\pi$.
Rather than relying on 3D ground truths, we leverage a pre-trained 2D editor $\mathcal{E}_\phi$ to refine $x^{\texttt{src}}$ into a higher-fidelity target $x^{\texttt{tgt}}$.
The optimization objective is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:task}
\theta^* = \arg\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{x^{\texttt{ref}}, \pi}
\left[\mathcal{L}(x^{\texttt{src}}, x^{\texttt{tgt}})\right]
\end{equation}
\[
x^{\texttt{src}} = \mathcal{P}(G_\theta(x^{\texttt{ref}}), \pi), \quad
x^{\texttt{tgt}} = \mathcal{E}_\phi(x^{\texttt{src}}, x^{\texttt{ref}})
\]
where $\mathcal{L}$ is a supervision loss that encourages better visual fidelity.


\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}
\textbf{Flow Matching Framework and Notation.}
Flow Matching models generation as an ODE process that transports Gaussian noise to the data distribution:
\begin{equation}
    dx_t = v(x_t; t, c) dt, \quad t \in [1, 0]
\end{equation}
where $x_t$ is the latent state at time $t$, and $c$ is the condition (e.g., text or image).
For better alignment to the condition $c$, Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is applied to the velocity field, using a null condition $\emptyset$ and a guidance scale $w$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cfg_guidance}
    v^{w}(x_t; t, c) = v(x_t; t, \emptyset) + w \cdot (v(x_t; t, c) - v(x_t; t, \emptyset))
\end{equation}
A key property used in our method is single-step estimation of the clean sample $\hat{x}_0$ from the intermediate state $x_t$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:single_step_estimation}
    \hat{x}_0 = x_t - t \cdot v(x_t; t, c)
\end{equation}
To distinguish modalities, we use $z$ and $\theta$ for the 3D generator $v_\theta$, where $G_\theta$ denotes its ODE sampling process.
Similarly, we use $x$ and $\phi$ for the 2D editor $v_\phi$, where $\mathcal{E}_\phi$ denotes the multi-step editing process as described below.


\textbf{Inversion-Free Image Editing.}
We leverage FlowEdit, an inversion-free method that modifies images by coupling source and target flow trajectories.
Starting from $x^{\texttt{src}}$, it iteratively updates the editing variable via $x^{\texttt{edit}} \leftarrow x^{\texttt{edit}} - \Delta t \cdot \tilde{v}$, where the differential velocity is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:flowedit_transition}
    \tilde{v} = v_\phi^{w_{\texttt{tgt}}}(x^{\texttt{tgt}}(t); t, c^{\texttt{tgt}}) - v_\phi^{w_{\texttt{src}}}(x^{\texttt{src}}(t); t, c^{\texttt{src}})
\end{equation}
where the \textbf{source branch} and the \textbf{target branch} is constructed as
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:flowedit_states}
    x^{\texttt{src}}(t) = (1-t)x^{\texttt{src}} + t\epsilon, \quad x^{\texttt{tgt}}(t) = x^{\texttt{edit}} + (x^{\texttt{src}}(t) - x^{\texttt{src}}), \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I).
\end{equation}
The editing process is performed over the last $N_e$ steps of the full $N$-step ODE schedule.
With integration step size $\Delta t = t_{i-1} - t_i$, $x^{\texttt{edit}}$ evolves into $x^{\texttt{tgt}}$, which transfers the image from being consistent with $c^{\texttt{src}}$ to following $c^{\texttt{tgt}}$ without the need for costly inversion that maps the image back to noise, thereby preserving structural fidelity.


\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \caption{Reinforced Editing}
    \label{alg:red}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require Rendered view $x^{\texttt{src}}$, Reference condition $c^{\texttt{ref}}$, Editor $\mathcal{E}_\phi$, Guidance scale $w$, Editing steps $N_e$, Total steps $N$
    \Ensure Pseudo-GT $x^{\texttt{tgt}}$
    \State Initialize $x^{\texttt{edit}} \leftarrow x^{\texttt{src}}$, Sample noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$
    \State Select time steps $\{t_i\}_{i=0}^{N_e}$ as the last $N_e$ steps from the full schedule of $N$ steps
    \For{$i = 0$ to $N_e-1$} \Comment{\texttt{FlowEdit: Backward Euler Integration}}
        \State $t \leftarrow t_i$, $\Delta t \leftarrow t_{i+1} - t_i$
        \State $x^{\texttt{src}}(t) \leftarrow (1-t)x^{\texttt{src}} + t\epsilon$
        \State $x^{\texttt{tgt}}(t) \leftarrow x^{\texttt{edit}} + (x^{\texttt{src}}(t) - x^{\texttt{src}})$ \Comment{\texttt{Eq.~\ref{eq:flowedit_states}}}
        
        \Statex \texttt{// Reinforced Editing (Sec.~\ref{sec:reinforced_editing})}
        \State $\tilde{v} \leftarrow v_\phi^{w}(x^{\texttt{tgt}}(t); t, c^{\texttt{ref}}) - v_\phi^{-w}(x^{\texttt{src}}(t); t, c^{\texttt{ref}})$ \Comment{\texttt{Eq.~\ref{eq:red_diff_velocity}}}
        \State $x^{\texttt{edit}} \leftarrow x^{\texttt{edit}} - \tilde{v} \Delta t$ \Comment{\texttt{FlowEdit: Euler Step}}
    
        \Statex \texttt{// Noise Update}
        \State $\epsilon \leftarrow \epsilon - (v_\phi(x^{\texttt{tgt}}(t); t, c^{\texttt{ref}}) - v_\phi(x^{\texttt{tgt}}(t); t, \emptyset)) \cdot (1-t)$ \Comment{\texttt{Eq.~\ref{eq:red_dynamic_noise}}}
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $x^{\texttt{tgt}} \leftarrow x^{\texttt{edit}}$
    \end{algorithmic}
    \end{algorithm}


\subsection{Constructing On-the-fly Pseudo-GTs via Reinforced Editing}
\label{sec:reinforced_editing}

To construct the supervision target $x^{\texttt{tgt}}$, we propose Reinforced Editing, a specialized adaptation of FlowEdit designed to inject realistic details while preserving the original geometric structure.
Unlike general-purpose editing, our goal is to enhance visual fidelity without altering the camera perspective or object pose.

To achieve this, we build on the FlowEdit differential velocity in Eq.~\ref{eq:flowedit_transition} and modify the flow trajectory by applying opposite guidance scales.
Instead of transitioning between different conditions, we use the same reference condition $c^{\texttt{ref}}$ for both the source and target branches.
The differential velocity $\tilde{v}$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:red_diff_velocity}
    \tilde{v} = v_\phi^{w}(x^{\texttt{tgt}}(t); t, c^{\texttt{ref}}) - v_\phi^{-w}(x^{\texttt{src}}(t); t, c^{\texttt{ref}}),
\end{equation}
By pushing away from the unconditional score via $-w$ and pulling towards the reference-conditioned score via $w$, this mechanism effectively reinforces the visual details of $c^{\texttt{ref}}$ while neutralizing structural drift, ensuring the editing variable $x^{\texttt{edit}}$ remains geometrically aligned with $x^{\texttt{src}}$.


\textbf{Noise Update.}
To reduce stochastic variance and stabilize the editing trajectory, we dynamically update the noise term $\epsilon$ at each step instead of resampling it.
We mitigate this by updating $\epsilon$ via:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:red_dynamic_noise}
    \epsilon \leftarrow \epsilon - (v_\phi(x^{\texttt{tgt}}(t); t, c^{\texttt{ref}}) - v_\phi(x^{\texttt{tgt}}(t); t, \emptyset)) \cdot (1-t)
\end{equation}
This update effectively injects the classifier guidance term in Eq.~\ref{eq:cfg_guidance} into the noise, reducing variance and preserving fine-grained structural details.


\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \caption{Closed-loop Optimization via Dynamic Pseudo-GTs}
    \label{alg:oreo}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require Generator $G_\theta$, Frozen rollout generator $G_{\theta_{pre}}$, 2D editor $\mathcal{E}_\phi$, Dataset $\mathcal{S}$, Learning rate $\eta$
    \Ensure Optimized parameters $\theta^*$
    \While{not converged}
        \State Sample $x^{\texttt{ref}} \sim \mathcal{S}$, $\pi$, and $t \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)$
        \State $z_0 = G_{\theta_{pre}}(x^{\texttt{ref}})$ \Comment{\texttt{Rollout}: Generate clean latent}
        \State $z_t = (1-t)z_0 + t\epsilon,\ \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ \Comment{\texttt{Noise Injection}}
        \State Predict $\hat{z}_0$ from $z_t$ using $G_\theta$ \Comment{\texttt{Prediction}: Denoise latent}
        \State $x^{\texttt{src}} = \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}(\hat{z}_0), \pi)$ \Comment{\texttt{Render}: Decode and project}
        \State Obtain $x^{\texttt{tgt}}$ via Algorithm~\ref{alg:red} from $x^{\texttt{src}}$ and $x^{\texttt{ref}}$ \Comment{\texttt{Reinforced Editing}}
        \State $\mathcal{L}_{sup} = \| x^{\texttt{src}} - x^{\texttt{tgt}} \|^2$ \Comment{\texttt{Supervision}}
        \State $\mathcal{L}_{reg} = \| v_\theta(z_t) - v_{\theta_{pre}}(z_t) \|^2$ \Comment{\texttt{Regularization}}
        \State $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \nabla_\theta (\mathcal{L}_{sup} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{reg})$ \Comment{\texttt{Update parameters}}
    \EndWhile
    \end{algorithmic}
    \end{algorithm}
    


\subsection{Generator Optimization via Pseudo-GT Supervision}
\label{sec:optimization}


We optimize the 3D generator $G_\theta$ in a closed-loop manner, leveraging dynamically generated pseudo-GTs as supervision.
In each training iteration, we first sample a clean latent $z_0 = G_{\theta_{pre}}(x^{\texttt{ref}})$ from the frozen pre-trained generator and perturb it with Gaussian noise at a random timestep $t \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)$ to obtain $z_t = (1-t)z_0 + t\epsilon$.
Given $z_t$, the trainable generator predicts the velocity field $v_\theta(z_t; t, c)$, and the denoised latent is estimated by
\begin{equation}
\hat{z}_0 = z_t - t \cdot v_\theta(z_t; t, c),
\end{equation}
which is decoded and rendered with a sampled camera pose $\pi$ as $x^{\texttt{src}} = \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}(\hat{z}_0), \pi)$.
To enhance visual fidelity, we generate a pseudo-GT $x^{\texttt{tgt}}$ from $x^{\texttt{src}}$ using Reinforced Editing in Algorithm~\ref{alg:red}.
The supervision loss is defined as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{sup} = \| x^{\texttt{src}} - x^{\texttt{tgt}} \|^2
\end{equation}
and backpropagated through the differentiable renderer and decoder to update $\theta$.
To preserve geometric stability, we regularize the velocity field deviation from the pre-trained prior:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{reg} = \| v_\theta(z_t) - v_{\theta_{pre}}(z_t) \|^2
\end{equation}
where $v_{\theta_{pre}}$ is the velocity field induced by the frozen pre-trained generator.
The final objective is $\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{sup} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{reg}$.
The overall training procedure is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:oreo}.

\subsection{Preliminary Analysis: Validating the Editing Feedback}
\label{4_1}

Before integrating the 2D editor into the 3D optimization loop, we first evaluate the quality of its feedback on an independent 2D benchmark.

\textbf{Test Set.}
We collect 100 images covering imaginative and difficult user scenarios to serve as reference images $x^{\texttt{ref}}$.
To obtain the source images $x^{\texttt{src}}$, we use Trellis to generate 3D assets from these references and render 3 views at azimuth $-90^\circ$, $0^\circ$, and $90^\circ$ for each asset.

\textbf{Compared Methods.}
We implement our Reinforced Editing (RE) algorithm using Qwen-Image-Edit as the base model, and compare it against the original Qwen-Image-Edit pipeline and NanoBanana.



\textbf{Qualitative Analysis.}
Fig.~\ref{fig:edit_comparison} visualizes the feedback quality. A critical challenge is maintaining the camera perspective of the source view $x^{\texttt{src}}$.
The original Qwen-Image-Edit often fails to respect the source viewpoint, either reverting to the reference view $x^{\texttt{ref}}$ or hallucinating an arbitrary angle, which introduces severe geometric conflicts.
NanoBanana, while more stable, tends to over-smooth details.
In contrast, Reinforced Editing injects high-fidelity textures while strictly adhering to the source perspective, ensuring geometrically aligned supervision.

\textbf{Quantitative Results.}
We use CLIP and DINO embedding similarity to measure semantic similarity, and Mask IoU to measure viewpoint and structural consistency.
The \textit{Baseline} row represents the unedited rendered view $x^{\texttt{src}}$, quantifying its initial alignment with the reference $x^{\texttt{ref}}$.
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:feedback_comparison}, baseline editors can achieve high similarity to $x^{\texttt{ref}}$ but deviate substantially from $x^{\texttt{src}}$, which is evidenced by their low Mask IoU.
In contrast, RE maintains high similarity to $x^{\texttt{src}}$ while still improving semantic fidelity.



\textbf{Ablation Study on RE Parameters.}
After selecting RE as the feedback source, we further study its key hyper-parameters: the editing ratio $N_e/N$ and the absolute editing steps $N_e$, as discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:reinforced_editing}.
We sample 50 rendered views from the test set and report CLIP Similarity, DINO Similarity, and Mask IoU.
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_ratio_steps}(a) and Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_vis}(a), a ratio of 0.75 provides the best balance between semantic injection and structural preservation.
Lower ratios limit editing plasticity, while full editing 1.0 destroys the original geometry.
Furthermore, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_ratio_steps}(b) and Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_vis}(b), increasing editing steps beyond 9 yields diminishing returns in semantic gain while significantly increasing computational cost.
Therefore, we use $N_e=9$, $N=12$ for all subsequent 3D experiments.

\subsection{Main Results}

\textbf{Setup.}
For 3D evaluation, we use Trellis-Image-Large as the pre-trained backbone and Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 as the 2D editor.
To address the lack of high-fidelity benchmarks in existing 3D datasets, we curate a High-Fidelity 3D Evaluation Set of 100 complex objects with intricate textures and diverse geometries.
We evaluate the performance of OREO against the original pre-trained Trellis baseline on this test set.

\textbf{Effectiveness of Reinforced Editing.}
We first validate the necessity of using Reinforced Editing (RE) to construct pseudo-GTs.
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_overview}, directly using standard FlowEdit without our modifications (i.e., w/o differential velocity and noise update) leads to severe geometric inconsistencies, as the editor tends to hallucinate new structures or change the viewpoint.
In contrast, OREO with full RE successfully injects realistic details while strictly preserving the original geometry, providing reliable supervision for the 3D generator.

\textbf{Impact of Regularization.}
We also analyze the role of the regularization term $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$.
Without regularization, the generator may overfit to high-frequency artifacts in the pseudo-GTs, leading to noisy surfaces.
Adding $\mathcal{L}_{reg}$ effectively smooths the geometry and ensures that the optimization trajectory remains close to the plausible 3D manifold.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper proposes \textbf{OREO}, a fidelity alignment framework for 3D generation.
Addressing the lack of visual realism in existing 3D models, we introduce an on-the-fly optimization loop that leverages 2D diffusion priors.
At its core, we propose Reinforced Editing to generate structure-preserving pseudo-GTs, which serve as dynamic supervision targets.
Experimental results demonstrate that OREO effectively enhances the visual fidelity and texture details of 3D generators while maintaining geometric integrity, offering a scalable solution to bridge the gap between 3D geometry and 2D visual realism.

\textbf{Future Work.} Although OREO performs well, there is still room for further exploration. First, the current FlowEdit process has high inference costs; future work could explore more efficient distillation strategies (e.g., one-step editing). Second, we will attempt to extend OREO to more complex scene generation tasks, utilizing panoramic editing models to optimize large-scale 3D environments. Finally, combining with Large Multimodal Models (LMM) for more fine-grained interactive editing is also an exciting direction.
